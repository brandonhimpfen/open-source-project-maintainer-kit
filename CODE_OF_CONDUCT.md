# Community Code of Conduct

## Purpose

[PROJECT NAME] aims to maintain a constructive environment for collaboration. Participants in project-controlled spaces are expected to communicate professionally and treat others with respect.

## Expected conduct

Participants should:

- keep criticism focused on ideas, code, documentation, and project decisions rather than individuals;
- provide feedback that is relevant and constructive;
- respect reasonable differences in experience, perspective, and communication style;
- avoid deliberately disrupting project discussions or workflows;
- respect privacy and avoid publishing another person's private information without permission; and
- follow project rules and reasonable maintainer directions intended to keep collaboration productive.

## Unacceptable conduct

The project does not accept harassment, threats, targeted intimidation, discriminatory abuse, sexual harassment, deliberate disclosure of private information, sustained personal attacks, or repeated disruptive behavior.

Technical disagreement, criticism, and rejection of a proposal are not by themselves misconduct. Participants should keep disagreements focused on the work and project needs.

## Scope

This policy applies to spaces controlled by [PROJECT NAME], including its repositories, issue trackers, project discussions, and project-managed communication channels. It may also apply when a person is clearly acting in an official project capacity.

## Reporting concerns

Report conduct concerns privately through `[CONTACT METHOD]`.

Provide relevant context where appropriate, but avoid including unnecessary sensitive personal information.

## Response

Those responsible for project moderation may review a report, request additional context, remove content, restrict participation, issue a warning, temporarily limit access, or permanently remove a participant from project-controlled spaces when reasonably necessary.

Responses should consider the conduct, its impact, relevant history, and the project's practical ability to manage the situation.

Reports should be handled as discreetly as reasonably possible, recognizing that limited information may need to be shared to investigate or respond.

## Conflicts

If a report concerns the person normally responsible for handling reports, use `[ALTERNATE CONTACT OR PROCESS]`.

## Good-faith reporting

Knowingly false or malicious reports may themselves be treated as misconduct. A report that cannot be substantiated is not, by itself, a false or malicious report.
